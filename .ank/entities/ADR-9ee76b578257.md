Raised by issue #503. A foundation ADR recording several decisions, three of
which a later ADR replaces or amends, has today two expressions and both are
wrong. `--supersedes` retires the whole of it, so `archive` treats as cold nine
decisions that still hold. An identifier in the body is prose, and prose
confers nothing (ADR-1e6bcbf62e61): `check` does not resolve it and `show` does
not surface it, so a reader of the amended ADR has no way to learn that part of
it no longer holds.

This corpus has met the case itself and answered it by supersession with the
text carried over: ADR-c88f99e1c16e changes one clause of ADR-5a690829388d and
carries the rest word for word. That answer works for an ADR short enough to
copy, and it costs a second copy of every clause that did not change, which is
the copy ADR-e3cb36646d77 warns about. For an ADR of twelve decisions it is not
an answer.

**Why a declared field, and not a reference.** `--reference` is refused on an
ADR because an ADR binds rather than cites, and that refusal stays: a citation
says "I rest on this", an amendment says "this no longer holds as written".
Folding the second into the first would make every reader of a reference ask
which of the two it is.

**Why the amended ADR stays accepted.** Its ratification anchors its text, and
nothing in the amendment rewrites that text; the reader holds both, the way the
reader of a superseded reference follows the chain (ADR-c88f99e1c16e). What
changes is what `show` prints beside it, so the partial invalidation is visible
where the reader already looks.

**What remains for the implementation to settle**, without reopening this: the
field's name in the schema, whether a schema bump is needed, and how `context`
presents an amended ADR within the budget.
