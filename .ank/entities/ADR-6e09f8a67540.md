constraint: |
  An ADR declares the smallest perimeter its constraint governs, and a glob covering a whole crate is declared where the constraint governs the whole crate and not otherwise. Nothing is refused and no scope is judged: new and amend name what a declared scope costs, as the characters of constraint it adds to each open task the scope covers, so a perimeter is priced when it is chosen rather than by whoever claims a task afterwards. Where check reports an over-constrained scope, the charge names first the constraints whose scope is wider than the perimeter the task declares and only then those that are merely long, because a perimeter is what a reader can act on and a length is not. A ratified ADR's scope is anchored in its ratification commit and this reaches none of them: what is declared stays declared, and this binds what is written after it.

Measured on this corpus on 2026-08-23, before deciding. A task whose scope
names one file, crates/ank-cli/src/human.rs, is handed 27 constraints
totalling 18745 characters against a limit of 4000, which is half of
context_budget. Every one of the five open tasks carries that signal. Not a
majority of them: all of them.

**The limit is not the thing that is wrong.** Six of the 27 name the files
they govern. Those six weigh 4415 characters, which is a limit of 4000 doing
its job. The other 21 declare a glob over a whole crate and weigh 14330, or
76 percent of the charge. Three of the 21 are legitimately transversal, the
language, the project name and the licence, and weigh 1530 between them.
That leaves 18 decisions and 12800 characters that say crates/ank-cli/** while
governing claim, or check, or a listing. ADR-4e7c25b1f639 puts claims in git
refs and declares itself over style.rs.

**Raising the limit would silence the thermometer.** It is the reading that
gets more expensive, not the measure that is miscalibrated, and the six
precise scopes are the proof: at 4415 they sit where the rule expects a
healthy perimeter to sit.

**The practice is getting worse, and recently.** Between 2026-08-08 and
2026-08-20 six ADRs named their files. Between 2026-08-21 and 2026-08-22
seven were ratified and none did. The charge rose 1287 characters in a single
session. Nobody decided the number should grow; it grows because the broad
glob is what costs the writer least and the reader most, and the writer is
the one holding the pen.

**Most of it can never be repaired, which is why this is about what comes
next.** A ratified ADR's scope is anchored in its ratification commit and no
amend reaches it. Correcting 18 globs would mean superseding 18 decisions to
fix a path, and this corpus has already met that wall on a docs path and
concluded that rewriting the scope of settled entities falsifies the register
rather than repairing it. So nothing here reaches backwards. What it does is
stop the bleeding, and put the price where the choice is made.

**The charge is sorted by length today, and that sends the reader at the
wrong target.** check names ADR-3094538d831e as the one that costs the most.
ADR-3094538d831e is one of the six that name their files. It is expensive
because it is long, not because it is wide, and the note advises mechanising
it out of injected context, which returns 1640 of the 14745 characters that
would have to go. A reader following that advice works on the group that is
already doing the right thing. Sorting by perimeter first puts the 18 in
front, where at least the ones still proposed can be changed.

**Nothing is refused, here as everywhere.** A wide perimeter is sometimes
correct and the tool is not the judge of that. What it can do is say what the
perimeter costs at the moment somebody chooses it, instead of leaving it to
be discovered by whoever claims a task weeks later and is handed 27
constraints for one file.

**This decision pays its own price and declares three files rather than a
crate.** It adds its constraint to the charge of every task those three
files, and an ADR about the cost of perimeters that declared crates/ank-cli/**
would be an argument against itself.
