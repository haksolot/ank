The conformance test of TASK-155e98c184ed checks each fixture against the shape
its verb declares, and says out loud what it cannot check: fourteen element
shapes sit under arrays that are empty in every fixture, so their declarations
rest on the builders in the source and on nothing the test can see.

    show.blocked_by            status.also_held        context.constraints
    show.unblocks              status.elsewhere        context.specs
    show.detached_proofs       review.live             scope.specs
    show.log                   graph.edges             log-read.entries
    check.findings[].charge    help.verbs[].refuses

The list is asserted verbatim rather than counted, so this task is what turns it
from a standing exemption into a closed one. Each row needs a corpus that
produces it: a task blocked by another for the two edge lists, an attested proof
on a ref for `detached_proofs`, a second live claim for `also_held`, a claim on
origin for `elsewhere`, an accepted ADR for `context.constraints`, a spec in
scope for the two `specs`, an over-constrained perimeter for `charge`.

`help.verbs[].refuses` is the odd one and may not be reachable: the empty ones
are the verbs that refuse on nothing, and a fixture cannot make `context` refuse.
If that is so, the honest end is the assertion naming that one alone, with the
reason -- not a relaxed check.
