`ank new spec` is the one wrong subcommand somebody actually types, because a
tool that stores tasks and decisions looks like a tool that stores
specifications. The answer today is `unknown subcommand`, which is true and
teaches nothing: the reader concludes the kind is missing and worth adding,
where the record says it was proposed, weighed and refused.

The reasoning is not lost, it is only unreachable from the keyboard.
TASK-00660963bcce carries it, and section 10 of the specification carries the
row and the trigger: a specification's authority comes from being one coherent
document, its sections are not independent the way ADRs are, and fragmenting
them creates between the fragments the drift the single document prevents. The
remedy available today needs no code, and it is a command: distil the section
into a scoped ADR that summarises it and points back at it.

So this is the self-correcting error the style asks for, applied to the one case
where the correction is a decision rather than a typo. Name the record, name the
section, and let the hint be `ank new adr` with its flags, which is the thing to
run next.

Nothing else moves. Every other unknown subcommand keeps the message it has,
because for those the kind really is absent and there is nothing to cite. The
kind registry stays as it is: this task adds no kind, and the row in section 10
still says not to anticipate one.
