Found while executing TASK-a12553192afa and left out of it, whose criterion
asked the two documents themselves to state this. They cannot: `ank edit`
refuses a ratified spec with exit 6, "its body and scope are anchored in its
ratification commit", and names the route instead. A ratified document is
replaced and never edited, so the revision is two new entities and two
ratifications, which is a different act from the one that changed the code.

**What the specification says today that the binary no longer does.** §4 opens
with "Global flags, deliberately limited to three", and there are four. Its
short-form tail says "The three globals take their letter ahead of everything
else", and the letterless list beside it does not carry `--worktree` (`w`).
That is the half of this task that is a correction rather than an addition, and
it is why the task exists at all rather than waiting for someone to notice.

§6 is the other half and it is an omission, not an error: "Resolving the
repository" describes a walk that answers one question, and there are two.
Everything it says stays true and none of it is rewritten; what it gains is the
anchor, and the four confrontations that move with it.

**`ank check` will not catch either.** It resolves a spec's references and
verifies coherence between documents, not prose against a binary. Nothing turns
red while this is open, which is exactly the argument for writing it down as a
task rather than trusting it to be remembered.

`ank new spec --supersedes` is the whole mechanism, and the reference lists of
both documents have to travel: a superseding spec that drops a citation loses
the anchor for whatever cited it.
