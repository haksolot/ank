Measured on 2026-09-14: 1921 entity files, 4.57 MB. Superseded specs are
32 files and 1.77 MB, 39 percent of the bytes: a spec amendment copies a
whole document of about 55 KB and the copy is immutable forever
(ADR-6b3f19e08a24). Log entries are 1394 files and 1.12 MB, 24 percent of the
bytes and 73 percent of the files: every session writes them and no rule
moves them off the path every verb walks. August 2026 alone added 1645
entities and 3.77 MB. Both mechanisms are unbounded by construction, and
the readers pay per file (the scan) and per byte (`check`, the cold
rebuild, every CI job).

## Why an archive directory, and not a packfile or git history

The format's promise is one file per entity, readable with no parser and
no tool (SPEC-aa4b §6: flat and fixed, or the format is not a format). A
packfile breaks it; git history breaks it for a reader with no git, which
§6 names as a reader the format serves. A second flat directory keeps the
promise whole and costs the store what it already pays for the previous
layout: two roots, canonical first. The archive is read on demand and
never by default, which is what makes moving a file there change what a
verb costs without changing what `show` answers.

## Why tasks stay hot

A done task is the record `check`, `attest` and the proof refs point at,
and the freeze of its criterion is what `done` is anchored to. Moving it
would move the thing every anchor names. Its trace can go: `log <id>`
resolves into the archive, and the entries of a task done on the default
branch are read by whoever asks and by nothing that runs on every verb.

## Why a budget

The attention budget of §5 turned a silent overflow into a finding that
names the fact the corpus outgrew. Weight gets the same treatment: a
number declared in `config.yml`, a signal when it is crossed, and the
verb that resolves it in the message. Bloat stops being something a
reader discovers by timing a verb.

## What lands where

`ank archive` writes files and no commit (§12), like `done`; the move lands
by pull request like a ratification, because it is a human deciding what
the hot corpus is. `find --all` is the one flag: a listing answers a
program whole (ADR-3e6ce108edcd), and the archive is part of the whole when
asked for.
