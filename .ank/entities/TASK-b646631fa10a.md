Measured 2026-09-14, release build of 2a65d0c, `graph --json` with no
`index.db`: 0.7 s at 960 entity files, 2.1-2.4 s at 1921, 8.7-9.0 s at 3842.
Doubling the corpus quadruples the rebuild. Every fresh clone, every
worktree with a corpus of its own and every CI job pays it once.

**The mechanism, reproduced outside ank.** `upsert` (`index.rs:1058`) runs
`DELETE FROM entities_fts WHERE id IN (SELECT id FROM entities WHERE path =
?1)` and `DELETE FROM entities_fts WHERE id = ?1`; `forget` (`index.rs:1044`)
has the first shape. FTS5 answers a WHERE on a column with no MATCH and no
rowid by scanning the whole virtual table, so the n-th insert scans the n-1
rows before it. With Python's sqlite3 and the same statements: at n=4000 the
inserts alone cost 0.09 s and the inserts with the two deletes 12.9 s; at
n=1000, 0.02 s against 0.64 s.

**The shape.** `entities` gains `rid INTEGER PRIMARY KEY` (explicit, since an
implicit rowid may be renumbered by VACUUM) and `id` becomes `TEXT NOT NULL
UNIQUE`; `entities_fts` rows are inserted with `rowid = rid` and deleted by
`rowid`, resolved through `entities` by path or id first. `SCHEMA_VERSION`
goes 6 to 7 (`index.rs:41`): every index is wiped and rebuilt once, which
is the cost this task makes linear. A ratio and not a wall, because a
runner's clock is not the same number twice (ADR-cc65f1388a71); the ratio
today is about 4 at these sizes.
