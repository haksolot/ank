## Context

Measured on 2026-10-26 with virgin agents (no skill, no hook, no memory) on a
fixture corpus: every agent that typed a help command typed `ank --help` first,
and some went on to `ank context --help` and `ank claim --help`. All of them
received `error[1]: unknown command '--help'` and spent a turn reaching
`ank help`, which then taught them the loop without the skill. That is three
agents out of three over two rounds, Opus and Haiku alike.

SPEC-7901 §9 asks for "never generic help, always the exact command to run
next". The refusal does name a next command, but the command the caller meant
is not in doubt: `--help` is the universal spelling of the question `ank help`
answers.

## Decision

`--help` and `-h`, alone or after a verb, are parsed as `ank help [<verb>]`.
They are not a new verb and not a flag of any verb: `ank help` keeps listing
exactly the verbs it lists, and no verb's `flags:` line grows. Anything after
`--help` is ignored, so `ank done TASK-x --help` prints the help of `done` and
runs nothing.

## Consequences

ADR-24efa7543a16's frozen surface is untouched: the surface is the verbs `ank help`
prints, and this adds a spelling, not a verb. SPEC-7901 §9 gains one sentence
saying so, so the specification and the parser stay one description.
