Measured the first time a spec document was superseded (TASK-1e2e47327d71).
`SPEC-c33e07a82cc4` was replaced, and `check` reported four documents citing it.
Three were live and followed the chain with the command the finding named, which
is the mechanism of ADR-5a690829388d working exactly as designed.

The fourth was `SPEC-fa2f8c49dba4`, which had itself been superseded in the same
operation. Its successor already cites the new document, so the corpus is
coherent; what the finding asks for is that a **retired** document be edited to
point at something written after it was retired.

That is the wrong request, on this corpus's own terms. A superseded entity is
history: it records what was decided and what it rested on **at the time**, and
rewriting its citations would make it cite a document it never read. Worse, the
repair the finding names would work — `amend` reaches the references of any spec
— so a reader following instructions bumps the `version` of a document that is
supposed to be settled, which is the shape of an entity being edited after the
fact that `check` reports everywhere else.

The rule to add is narrow: **a citer that is itself superseded is not asked to
follow anything.** It is the same reading `check` already gives elsewhere to
entities that are done with — a `done` task is not asked to fix its dead scope,
because `amend` refuses it and a finding nobody can act on is one readers learn
to skip. Here the finding *can* be acted on, and that is what makes it worse
rather than better.

What must not change is the live case. A `proposed` or `accepted` document
citing a superseded one is exactly what the signal exists for, and the three
repairs above are the evidence that it earns its place.
