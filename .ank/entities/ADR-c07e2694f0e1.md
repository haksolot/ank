constraint: |
  The terminal reader is a full-screen application drawn with ratatui over crossterm. Those two dependencies are spent here and nowhere else in the workspace, and what they buy is stated rather than assumed: a panel layout, keystroke input in raw mode, terminal resize, and mouse events. No FFI enters this tree for any of it, on any platform.
  
  Input is a keystroke and no longer a line. Every command that only moves the screen is one key. Every verb that writes is reached through a confirmation that shows the exact command line about to be run and does not run it until the person confirms. That confirmation is the whole of the guarantee that no write happens without an explicit act, and the guarantee is stated over the verbs that write rather than over a list of them: it must be impossible to reach a spawned write without passing through it, and a verb added to the reader is inside that rule on the day it arrives. Which verbs may be spawned at all stays a list written in the code, measured against the key table and never generated from it: a gate derived from the thing it guards guards nothing.
  
  The reader answers a person who is not at a desk. Below a stated width the panels reflow to one column; a row is selectable by touch, and touching the row already selected opens it, so a document is reached by a thumb with no button to press. Every action the focused pane offers is reachable by touch, through one permanently visible target that opens the key list, and every line of that list is itself a target that runs the verb it names. No command anywhere requires a modifier chord. What is no longer required is that the offer be drawn at rest: a band of targets on every frame costs four rows of a screen that has twenty-four, and the reader least able to spend them is the one on the phone this clause was written for.
  
  A key is the verb it runs. Where the CLI declares a verb, the reader binds that verb's own initial to it and navigation takes what is left; the letters belong to the surface a person already learned at the shell. What the reader offers is read out of the contract's own verb table rather than transcribed beside it, so the keys, the forms and the key list cannot drift from the CLI they spell.
  
  Structure is drawn with box-drawing glyphs, and drops to ASCII on the terminal that declares it can render neither those nor colour. That probe is the terminal's own declaration and never NO_COLOR: refusing colour is not refusing glyphs, and a reader whose characters moved when the paint was taken away would make 'nothing is carried by colour alone' unmeasurable -- it is measured by drawing the same corpus with the paint and without it and finding the two frames identical character for character. The focus is carried by the weight of a rule and by the marker on a title, never by colour alone.
  
  What ADR-8bd76e8d7c4e fixed is untouched and this decision restates none of it: the reader reaches the corpus only by running the CLI with --json, it writes nothing the person at the keyboard did not ask for, it renews no claim on its own, and accept stays a signed human act it may drive and never perform. No browser reader, nothing under a viewer/ directory, no HTML page: a reader that a phone can drive is a terminal application reached from a phone, and never a step toward one that is not.

ADR-0b55983421dd is kept in three of its five paragraphs and rewritten in the
one that has been overtaken by the screen it produced. What it decided -- that
the reader is a terminal application, that no write happens without a
confirmation showing the command, that a phone is a reader and not an
afterthought -- is not at issue and is carried forward in its own words.

## What the frame actually spends

Measured on this corpus, from the built binary on a pseudo-terminal, at 150x45
and at 40x30.

At eighty columns and twenty-four rows the reader spends three rows on a
header, two rows and two columns per panel on an ASCII border, one row on a
note band that is never absent, one on a band of touch targets, and two on key
lines that are cut with `~` before they finish. That is roughly thirteen of
twenty-four rows on furniture, and the two lines teaching the keys do not fit
on the screen they are taught from.

At forty columns and thirty rows the arrangement is worse in a way the
arithmetic of TASK-dd9747e5e305 did not reach: three of the four panels are
closed, and a closed panel is two rows of border drawing nothing. Six rows of
the phone's thirty are rules around emptiness.

## The clause that has to move, and why it was right when it was written

"the actions available on the focused pane are drawn as visible targets and
never only as keys to know" was written against a real failure: a reader whose
only affordance is a key is a reader a thumb cannot drive, and the band was the
cheapest answer available at the time. It bought the phone its first way in.

What it did not price is that the band is drawn at rest, on every frame, at
every window, and that the window it costs the most on is the phone's. Four
rows of twenty-four is the tax; the thing being taxed is the reader the clause
exists to protect.

The replacement is not fewer affordances but a cheaper anchor for them. A row
that is already selected opens on a second touch, so the commonest act on a
phone -- read this document -- needs no button at all. One cell of the header
carries `?`, and it is a target; every line of the list it opens is a target
that runs the verb it names. The offer is complete, reachable and free at rest,
which the band was only ever the first two of.

## Why the letters go to the verbs

The reader today binds `a`, then reads a word: `a`, `c-l-a-i-m`, Enter. Every
letter it does bind is navigation, and no letter it binds is a verb the CLI
has. So a person who knows `ank claim` knows nothing about the reader, and a
person who learns the reader learns nothing transferable.

Binding `c` to claim and `l` to log costs `h`, `l`, `n` and `p` as movement
keys, which is a real loss and a smaller one: `j`, `k`, the arrows, Tab and the
digits are what a lazygit reader reaches for first, and they are untouched.

## Why the table is read out of the contract

There are five parallel key tables in this crate today and two tests binding
any of them. The proof they can disagree is on the screen: `?` omits `v`,
Space, every arrow and the way out. A surface generated from
`ank-contract`'s own verb table cannot drift from the CLI it spells, and this
crate already depends on that contract for its exit codes.

## The glyphs, and what is being spent

LOG-ed57116ba141 recorded two reasons for ASCII borders: structure is text on
every platform, and the terminal least likely to carry box-drawing glyphs is
the one on a phone. The first is ADR-1f70ce2c3eac's and that decision's scope
is the CLI's renderer, not this one. The second has been overtaken by the
phones people actually read from.

It also recorded a mechanical reason, and that one is real: an identifier scan
in crates/ank-cli/tests/tui.rs slices frames by byte, and a glyph after a
truncated identifier lands inside a char. The answer is to fix the scan, not to
keep the whole reader in ASCII to protect it.

The degradation stays honest: the terminal that has already said it cannot
render richly gets the ASCII set back, on the same probe that drops colour, so
there is one answer to "this terminal is poor" and not two.
