The floor ADR-a22cd3196529 rests on, and the two assertions in the criterion are
the two ways this is usually got wrong.

**The undeclared corpus that is never read** is how "declared and never
discovered" stops being prose. It is a rule stated twice in the corpus, by
ADR-621a7fd96ce1 and again by ADR-96174f1ac2b7, and the implementation that
violates it is the obvious and friendly one. A test that plants a corpus where a
scan would find it, and shows it stays unread, is what makes the friendly
implementation fail rather than ship.

**Byte for byte, warm against cold** is how a cache stops being trusted. The
index is a cache from which nothing is believed over the files, and the moment a
warm listing and a cold listing differ, the daemon has become a source of truth
nobody voted for. Equality of the two is the property, and it is measurable
directly.

**Two worktrees, one corpus** is the case a path key gets wrong and an identity
key gets right. This repository is developed in worktrees, so it is not a
hypothetical here.

This is the lowest priority of the four subjects planned together, by intent
rather than by dependency: it improves answers that are already fast enough on a
corpus this size. Nothing blocks it, and nothing should be rushed into it before
there is a reader that feels the difference.
