Ratifying a successor is the act that kills every citation of its
predecessor in the source, and it is the only act in the corpus whose damage
is invisible to the person performing it.

**Measured on this repository, twice in two days.** Accepting
ADR-f7dc76886db2 and SPEC-d3adbd77dca9 on 2026-08-22 left thirty-three
citations of their predecessors in nine files across three crates. Both
ratifications were correct, both were signed, and neither said anything. The
default branch went red on all three platforms at the next push, on a test
that had been green minutes before, and stayed red across the three pushes
that followed because nothing in the loop connects the two events. The
repair took no judgement worth the delay: thirty-one of the sites took the
successor's identifier and nothing else.

**The knowledge exists at the moment of the act and is thrown away.** accept
holds the predecessor's identifier, it has just written the transition that
made it superseded, and the working tree is right there. What is missing is
one walk and one message, not a mechanism.

**Warning and never refusing, because the ratification is not what is
wrong.** A citation left behind is stale source, and the decision is sound
the moment it is signed. Refusing on the state of the working tree would
make a correct human act fail on someone else's stale comment, and would
give the person holding the signing key a reason to look for a way around
it. The exit code does not move.

**accept and not check, and the reason is cost.** check runs on every edit
to the corpus and its per-entity cost is already the subject of a task filed
to measure it. Reading every tracked file on each of those runs would spend
that budget on a question that only has a new answer at a ratification.
accept is rare, human, signed, on the default branch, and already slow.

**This does not reopen ADR-c88f99e1c16e.** That decision refused to
re-point citations inside accept, because writing to nine entities in one
act would deposit nine machinery entries and pollute the trace the corpus
built to watch itself. A message writes nothing: no amend, no version, no
entry, no commit. What was refused was the repair performing itself, not the
tool saying where the repair is owed.

**Nor does it contradict ADR-1e6bcbf62e61.** That one holds a superseded
identifier silent in the prose of the corpus, because prose is where history
is written and ank show carries the chain. The source has no chain to
follow: a comment in a module header hands the next reader a constraint with
the authority of a decision record and no command attached. Same identifier,
opposite verdict, and the place is what separates them.

The test no_superseded_document_is_cited_in_the_workspace already states the
rule and is the falsification for it. What it cannot do is arrive on time.
