Third step. The suite from the blocking task is red; this task is what turns it
green, and nothing in the fixtures may be edited to get there — a golden bent to
fit the parser has stopped being a golden.

The registry is the substance. Today the kind is stated in `id.rs`, in
`model.rs`, in a `parse.rs` match and in two straight-line serializers that differ
only in which fields they emit and in what order. Collapse that to one table and
one serializer. The field order is data in the table, not control flow: it is what
makes the round-trip byte-identical and it is the single most likely thing to be
lost by rewriting the emitters as a generic loop.

Two properties are easy to drop in the collapse and both are load-bearing.
Optional fields are omitted and never emitted empty, which is what lets a schema 1
file survive a rewrite unchanged. And the scalar quoting predicate is exact — the
character list, the reserved words, the number test — reproduced in
`docs/format.md` for third parties. Neither is negotiable and both have tests;
run them early rather than at the end.

`log.rs` keeps its grammar entirely. The dash, the timestamp, the identity, the em
dash, the message: nothing about an existing entry is reinterpreted, only its
address changes. The one-line-diff assertion moves with it and stays.

`verified` is optional everywhere and its absence is never a fault. Do not make it
required for any kind, and do not let the actor convention become a parse error —
the specification puts that in `check`, and enforcing it here would lock the 96
pre-convention files out of their own format.

Schema 1 and schema 2 fixtures staying byte-identical through a rewrite is the
regression test for the whole task. If they move, the bump has silently become a
migration.
