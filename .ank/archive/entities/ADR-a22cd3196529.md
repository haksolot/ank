The request is the one git answers with `git daemon` and a fetch in the
background: several repositories, several agents, and a person who wants to see
what is happening across them without paying a network round trip per command.
The need is real. Almost every shape it could take is already refused by
decisions this corpus has ratified, and the value of writing this down is that
the refusals are collected in one place rather than rediscovered one at a time.

## What the corpus already settled, and this only carries

**It may not go looking.** ADR-621a7fd96ce1 says it twice over, and
ADR-96174f1ac2b7 repeats it: aggregation is declared and never discovered,
nothing walks a filesystem looking for corpora. A daemon that scanned a home
directory for `.ank` would be the single most natural implementation and the one
this project has already refused, in writing, before it was proposed.

**It may not key on a path.** Two worktrees of one repository share a corpus and
have different paths; two clones of different repositories sit at the same path
on two machines. The repository identity of ADR-621a7fd96ce1 is derived from the
root commit for exactly this, and a board that counted a worktree twice would be
wrong in the way that is hardest to notice.

**It may not arbitrate.** `refs/ank/*` is per repository. ADR-a1de673043b4 and
ADR-372b82af1ec7 both spell out what follows: writing never crosses a corpus
boundary, claims stay per repository, and a process speaking for several corpora
that pretended otherwise would be inventing an arbitration the refs cannot carry.
A daemon is a reader over many, and a writer of nothing.

**It may not renew.** ADR-0bb7ea8991bc says a claim is renewed by working, not by
reporting. A daemon holding claims alive for absent agents is the exact inversion
of that, and it is the first feature anybody would ask for.

## The one thing genuinely decided here

**No verb, under any protocol.** ADR-1713af205186 refused a curated subset of the
verbs exposed over a second protocol, and ADR-372b82af1ec7 kept that refusal
whole as the shape of what is allowed: a full-surface passthrough generated from
the dispatch table, or nothing. A daemon answering the questions a board finds
convenient -- who holds what, what is proposed, the shape of the graph -- would be
that curated subset wearing a different name, and it would be the third dispatch
path in a project that has spent its whole history reducing to one.

So the daemon gives readers speed and news, and gives them no answers. It keeps
the index warm, so the CLI a reader already runs returns sooner. It fetches
`refs/ank/*`, so what the CLI reports about other agents is current instead of
frozen at somebody's last manual fetch. It says when something changed, so a
reader repaints instead of polling. Every one of those improves an answer the CLI
gives; none of them is an answer the daemon gives.

## Why nothing may depend on it

The daemon is a cache and a courier, and both are optional by construction. A
verb that behaved differently when it was running would make an installation with
a daemon and one without into two products, and the one without is the one every
CI runner, every container and every agent uses. Its absence is therefore not a
degraded mode: it is the normal one, made slower.

## Why the fetch is the narrow one

A full `git fetch` would keep drift from the default branch accurate too, which
is tempting and is refused. A background process that updates branches in a
repository where somebody is working is a source of surprises a coordination tool
has no business being. `refs/ank/*` is the namespace this project owns, nobody
rebases onto it, and a stale one is precisely the thing that makes `status` lie
about who holds what.
