THE WIDTH IS DERIVED, NOT CHOSEN, and that is the load-bearing part. view.rs states ONE_COLUMN = ADDRESSED + 2 + FLOOR = 47, where ADDRESSED (33) is what a listing spends on a row to say WHICH row it is -- the cursor's two columns, the row number's seven, ten for a short identifier and twelve for a status, gutters included -- and FLOOR (12, lifted out of share()) is what the panel beside it keeps. share() gives the focused one of the pair 'width - FLOOR', its borders take two, so 47 is the last window where the focused panel can carry an identifier AND a status. At 46 the status is being cut, at which point the pair is two panels neither of which answers 'which row is this', and one column is the honest arrangement. A unit test holds the constant to that derivation from both ends: at ONE_COLUMN the focused panel's inside width IS ADDRESSED, and a row's title does begin two columns after it.

ONE COLUMN IS AN ACCORDION, and I arrived at it by arithmetic rather than taste. My first design stacked all four panels at their old sizes; at 40x24 that leaves the body panel four rows inside, body_over() spends six on 'what holds it / what binds it', and the document itself never reaches the screen at all -- a reader that draws a title and no body. So: the focused panel is open and the other three are SHUT (2 rows -- their top border with the number and name on it, and their bottom). That is bb43's own rule in the other axis: focus is where the width goes on a screen with width to give, and where the SCREEN goes on one without. At 40x24 the open panel gets ten rows, which is a body you can actually page.

THE HEIGHTS ARE SUBTRACTION AND THE CONSTRAINTS ARE Max. arrange() computes 'room = height - chrome' and 'open = room - 3*SHUT' itself rather than handing the solver four competing constraints, so the arrangement says what it means at every window. But they go in as Max and never Length, and that cost me a red test before I saw why: the confirmation band is a Length, Length outranks Max, and four Length panels at 20x24 squeeze the argv band until 'y runs it' falls off the bottom -- the exact thing d4a8's criterion forbids. A panel gives way to the band that is telling somebody something.

THE TAP IS arrange() RUN BACKWARDS, which is what bb43 left the seam for and it was exactly that. App::pointed resolves three regions and no fourth: a target under the panels is the key it carries, PRESSED (it calls App::press with that KeyEvent, so there is one vocabulary and no second road); a panel is that panel focused, with the row under the finger selected; anything else is not a control. Order matters inside tapped(): the row is resolved BEFORE focus_on and applied after, because focus decides the arrangement -- on a phone it decides which panel is open at all -- so resolving after moving would select whatever had slid under the finger. Same defect the confirmation avoids by freezing its argv.

THE GATE HELD, which is what 74ff warned about. A tap while a command waits goes through keys::confirming like any key: the two targets answer it and every other touch dismisses it. A scroll under a pending or an open prompt moves nothing. dependencies.rs still counts exactly one ank.act( in src/, in view.rs.

NO CHORD, ASSERTED OVER A DOMAIN INSTEAD OF A LIST. keys.rs gained mod table: family() is a TOTAL match on KeyCode so a crossterm release that adds a key stops the file compiling, every_key() enumerates every variant (ASCII and beyond as Char, F1-F35, both nested enums whole), every_modifier() is all 64 bit combinations, and four tests state the rule over typed(), confirming() and edit() at every focus. It forced one real change: Tab and BackTab now answer Command::Panel(focus.stepped(+-1)) rather than NextPanel, and Command::NextPanel is gone. NextPanel(-1) was the one VALUE in the table no bare key produced -- while the PLACE it reached had been one digit away since bb43. Naming the destination made the screen's behaviour and the table's arithmetic the same fact. A second test says the only modifier the table reads at all is Control and the only thing it reaches with it is the way out; every other way of holding a key is transparent.

6cd4's WARNING OBEYED, and it cost nothing: the one-column branch composes no rows of its own. It reuses App::panel, so every listing still reaches the screen through painted(&[Composed], ink). The action targets ARE new drawn content and they are chrome -- paragraph(&[String]) like the three bands around them -- because a target carries a key and a word and neither is a field the shared table has an opinion about. paint.rs's source scan is unchanged and green.

MEASURED THROUGH THE BINARY as CLAUDE.md and the criterion both demand: crates/ank-tui/tests/phone.rs drives the built ank on a pseudo-terminal at 40x30, sends ESC[<0;col;rowM (SGR, one-based, with the release after it) and reads the grid back. Four tests: the four panels are one column and each is reached by touching it; a tap on a row the session did not open on marks THAT row and only it, and a tap on the target reading [Enter open] puts that entity in the body panel; a touch off a target dismisses a waiting claim with refs/ank/* unmoved while a touch on [y run] lands it. The fourth is the one only the wire can answer -- ESC[?1006h goes out at startup and ESC[?1006l comes back on the way out. Without it the other three would pass on a build that never enabled capture at all, because a pseudo-terminal forwards whatever is written to it whether the program asked or not.

Whoever picks that up should know the number is 33 + 2 and that ADDRESSED is where it is written down.
